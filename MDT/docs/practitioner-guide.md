# Practitioner Guide

## Role of the practitioner

The practitioner helps translate observations into a meaningful, ethical growth process. The practitioner is not a judge, a diagnostician, or a dispenser of certainty.

The professional role is to:

- hold the client with dignity
- communicate clearly and respectfully
- explain confidence levels and unknowns
- support reflection without pressure
- refrain from deterministic conclusions

## Required practice standards

Practitioners should:

- distinguish what is observed from what is interpreted
- present evidence, context, and confidence together
- avoid overreaching beyond the boundaries of the framework
- encourage client agency and self-authorship
- document gaps and follow-up questions transparently

## Practitioner report sections

A practitioner report typically includes:

- observational notes
- confidence scoring
- alternative interpretations
- missing data
- follow-up questions
- referenced literature or research context
- recommendations for next steps

## Ethical posture

Practitioner work must remain human-centered and non-harmful. The report should help the client move toward clarity, safety, and sustainable growth—not shame, fear, or rigid categorization.
