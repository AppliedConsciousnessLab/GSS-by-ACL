# Confidence Framework

Every MDT observation is assigned a confidence level that reflects the quality and quantity of available information.

## Scale

### A — High Confidence

The observation is strongly supported by available evidence, repeated patterns, and clear context.

### B — Moderate Confidence

The observation is plausible and useful, but requires additional context or corroboration.

### C — Preliminary Observation

The finding may be informative, but it remains exploratory and should be treated as a hypothesis rather than a conclusion.

### D — Insufficient Information

The observation lacks enough evidence to support a meaningful conclusion.

## Important principle

Confidence describes the available evidence, not the value of the person.

A lower confidence score does not imply the person is unworthy or less capable. It simply signals that the current material is limited and should be held with humility.

## Practitioner use

Practitioners should:

- clearly label confidence in each report section
- avoid presenting preliminary observations as certainty
- separate observation from interpretation
- invite client reflection before making lifestyle recommendations
- track missing information and follow-up questions
