# MDT Methodology

## Purpose

MDT is designed to help a person understand themselves with greater clarity, gentleness, and usefulness.

The method combines observation, reflection, and daily practice in a single structured experience. Its goal is not to flatten a person into a category but to illuminate patterns that may support growth.

## Methodological posture

MDT follows an evidence-informed, non-diagnostic model.

It differentiates between:

- established science
- active research
- traditional observational systems
- interpretive frameworks
- metaphor and teaching models
- client self-report and lived experience

Any claim should be treated according to its evidence level and purpose.

## Core process

1. Observe
   - Gather information about the person in a respectful, structured way.
2. Contextualize
   - Place observations within a broader human and developmental context.
3. Interpret carefully
   - Use explanation, not certainty, when inference is involved.
4. Prioritize growth
   - Focus on practical next steps, not diagnosis or shame.
5. Support integration
   - Turn insight into habits, rituals, and reflection.

## Evidence-informed boundaries

MDT does not claim to replace licensed care. It is designed to support reflection and personal development while avoiding:

- medical diagnosis
- deterministic labeling
- reduction of a person to a facial pattern or trait
- confidence inflation where information is incomplete

## Client-centered application

The framework is meant to be practical, compassionate, and cultivated in context. A person can choose what resonates, what is useful, and what should be set aside.

The strongest MDT outcomes are built on trust, clarity, and measured action.
